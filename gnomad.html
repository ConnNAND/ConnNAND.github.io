Gnomad

Engine Used: Unity

Time Frame: September 2024 - Current

Link: Official JollyWare Website

About the Project

Gnomad is a 2D adventure platformer with "bouncing ball" combat. Explore the vibrant world of the Sunken Forest while encountering curious creatures and strange monsters, all in search of a way back home.

This project began 1 year before I joined the team as the rest of the group's senior capstone. After graduation, one member left the team and I came to fill their place as the rest of us are pushing for a full Steam release sometime in 2027.

While being a founding member of JollyWare as a company, my role in the project is as the sound design lead, though the nature of a small team leads me to working on many aspects of the game.

Accomplishments & Lessons Learned

The project itself has received several notable achievements, including getting 1st place in the Pocket Gamer Connect's "Big Indie Pitch" competition during GDC, 14th place in the Blue Ocean Games "Rising Tide" Challenge 2, as well as gaining several millions of views across social media platforms.

My contributions include creating sound effects for various game elements and integrating their playback functionality depending on what is needed, including the system for dynamic combat music that changes based on the enemies present in the encounter.

In addition, I have also been responsible for a majority of the level design present in the current build of the game, planning and assembling the tutorial rooms, deciding the structure of the Steam demo, as well as designing the layouts of 2/3 available zones in the full game, including creating the environmental puzzles present in them.

On top of these, I've done work prototyping and making alterations to the game's enemies, notably refactoring Gorb AI to include a dash attack, creating the initial Grubfly prototype, and the Dunderhead.

I am also responsible for the shop interface, not in design, but in functionality.

Additionally, I have implemented the systems for various quests in the game, as well as accompanying dialogue and "flavor text" for the characters, quest descriptions, and item/cosmetic descriptions.

Early on, I faced the challenge of acquainting myself with the existing codebase and development workflows. While initially overwhelming, gradually through working on smaller tasks across different parts of the project, I was able to gain a better understanding of the codebase and become a valuable member of the team.

There are many lessons I have learned throughout this experience, including the importance of communication and collaboration. There's no reason to work in isolation if you can get the job done quicker and better by getting the right people involved when you have questions.

Working on a project for such an extended period has also shown the value of persistence. Eventually the "honeymoon" period passes no matter what you're working on, making sitting down to work harder, especially when there aren't exciting or interesting tasks at the moment. Knowing that someone has to do it or it won't get done at all helps to tear down the barrier to get started, and keeping the momentum is the most important thing you can do.
